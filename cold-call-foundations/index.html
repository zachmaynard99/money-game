Before any technique, know the scoreboard. A call where they liked you but nothing got booked doesn’t count. Primary goal: a qualified appointment with the decision maker. Everything else, rapport, intel, leaving the door open, is secondary. When you try something new on the phones, ask one question: did it book more qualified appointments? If yes, keep it. My line: enough volume removes the need for luck… and suck. You don’t have to be perfect if you make enough calls. 40 dials and 6 answers is not a skill problem, it’s a volume problem. IT’S HOW YOU SOUND

70% IS HOW YOU SOUND.

On the phone it’s 70% voice, 30% words. The best script in the wrong voice fails; an average script in the right voice wins.

Inflect down on facts. Up only on real questions.

Whatbox Digital · Appointment Setting Team

5

The 70/30 rule: about 70% of how you land is tone, pace, pauses, volume and inflection. Only 30% is the words. Smile when you greet them, they can hear it. Stand up or walk, never slump. After your name and reason for calling, pause for two beats. That pause signals control. Use downward inflection on facts so you sound certain. Save upward inflection for genuine questions.

TONE BY STAGE

NEVER STAY IN ONE TONE.

Shift your tone as the call moves. One tone sounds like a lecture; changing tone sounds like a person.

Match your tone to the stage of the call. Opening: curious and warm. Discovery: warm with some authority. Pitch: authority plus a bit of urgency. Close: authority with a falling cadence, so the time you suggest sounds final. Fast pace grabs attention but needs slowdowns. Slow adds weight. The pause is the most powerful beat you have.

KNOW WHO YOU’RE TALKING TO

FOUR OWNERS. FOUR WAYS TO PLAY IT.

Read them in the first 10 seconds: how fast they talk, the words they use, their tone. Then match them.

Whatbox Digital · Appointment Setting Team

7

Every agency owner is different. Most fall into one of four social styles, and you can usually read them in the first 10 seconds from their speed, their words and their tone. Match them; people trust people who feel like them. Driver / Director: fast, results-focused, decisive. Get to the point, lead with outcomes, don’t ramble. Analytical: data-driven and cautious, wants proof. Give specifics, numbers and the process. Don’t rush them or hype it. Expressive: big-picture, enthusiastic, talkative. Match their energy, tell stories, sell the vision, then steer back to booking the demo. Amiable: relationship and trust first, slower to decide. Be warm and patient, reassure them, and make the meeting feel safe.

FRAMES, NOT SCRIPTS

MEMORIZE THE BEATS.

Know each beat’s job and keep a few go-to phrases. Drill it: say the same call 5 different ways. Record and listen back.

Whatbox Digital · Appointment Setting Team

8

Scripts make you sound like you’re reading, and you panic the second they go off script. Scriptless doesn’t mean winging it. Memorize the beats. First, a pattern interrupt: say something that breaks their telemarketer autopilot, so they stop and actually listen instead of hanging up on reflex. Then the reason for the call and a value hook, then the ask. Keep Lego phrases ready: ‘Quick question for you…’, ‘Out of curiosity…’, ‘The reason I’m reaching out…’ Drill: say the same call five different ways inside the same frame. Record your calls. If you sound like you’re reading, you are.

IMPACT is the shape of the call: Intro, Motive, Positioning, Ask questions, Confirm and Close, Take next steps. Intro and motive: who you are and why you’re calling. Positioning: who we help and the outcome. Ask: how they bring in business today. Confirm and close: based on what they told you, recommend the demo. Next steps: lock the time and their details. It’s a frame, not a script, and the order can flex. It’s a conversation, so adapt. If you hit resistance at the close, go back to questions; you didn’t uncover the need yet.

There are two valid ways into a call. One: lead with a direct pitch. Short and clear: who we help, the outcome we get for agencies, and the ask for a demo. Two: lead with questions and find the pain first, then position the meeting as the fix. Neither is ‘right’. Try both, track them, and run whichever books the most qualified appointments for you.

When you say it, it’s suspect. When they say it, it’s gospel. Their words convince them, not ours. Use no-oriented questions; people feel safe saying no: ‘Is now a bad time?’, ‘Would it be a bad idea to set up a quick call?’, ‘Are you against exploring…?’ Pain ladder: surface symptom, then business impact (‘what does that do to your pipeline?’), then personal impact, then cost of doing nothing (‘what happens if nothing changes in 90 days?’). Mirroring: listen without interrupting and repeat their last few words back as a question. They’ll keep explaining. Keep discovery light: 2–3 smart questions, not 20. Afterwards ask yourself: did they admit the pain, or did I tell them?

SELL THE MEETING

SELL THE MEETING, NOT THE PRODUCT.

WHERE THEY ARE → WHERE THEY WANT TO BE.

Talk only about their pain today and the outcome they want.

Make the meeting feel safe and valuable: “Worst case, you walk away with ideas you can use.”

Whatbox Digital · Appointment Setting Team

12

We’re not selling the service on this call. We’re selling the next step. Coffee, not marriage. Think movie trailer, not the movie. Give them enough to be curious; tell them everything and they have no reason to meet. Talk about two things only: where they are now (the pain) and where they want to be (the outcome). Position the demo as the bridge. Make the meeting feel clear, safe and valuable. It’s not a ‘sales call’, it’s a quick chat to see how we can help. Always answer: what’s in it for them?

13

Words are only 30%, but they set the emotional frame. Use positive language: ‘I’ll keep this brief’ instead of ‘I won’t waste your time’; ‘Absolutely’ instead of ‘No problem’; ‘Let me explain it another way’ instead of ‘You don’t understand’. Sound like an insider. Agency owners talk about their book of business, policies and carriers. Build your own vocab list and use it. Cardinal sins: sounding like a telemarketer, over-talking (top reps talk about 43% of the time, prospects 57%), asking for marriage on the first date with price or contracts, arguing, and weak openers like ‘Sorry to bother you’ or ‘I just wanted to…’. Say ‘The reason for my call is…’ instead.

Objections are buying signals. They’re engaged. Silence is what kills calls. Expect about three hurdles before you get the appointment. Work every objection the same way: Listen (don’t interrupt, repeat their words), Empathize (tie it to what they said, no fake ‘I know how you feel’), Ask (an objection is a question in disguise: ‘When you say budget, do you mean cash flow now or return long term?’), then Answer with something tailored. Send me an email: ‘Happy to, but it’s tailored, so it’s hard to fit in an email. 10–15 minutes this week, and worst case you steal the ideas.’ Not interested: ‘Out of interest, what part of growing doesn’t interest you?’ Busy: ‘Completely understand, I’ll call back. Would 3 PM work?’ Already have someone: ‘Great, how’s it going? Has it brought real results? Could you be doing more?’ No budget: pivot to their goals; pricing gets covered on the demo.

Use a this-or-that close. ‘Great, we currently have a lot of calls booked with other agencies, but we have Wednesday or Thursday available. Which day works best?’ Then: ‘Wednesday we have 11am or 2pm. Which is best for you?’ If there’s a slot today, book today. If not, book tomorrow. The closer the meeting, the better it shows. GATEKEEPERS & SHOW-UPS

SOUND LIKE YOU’RE EXPECTED.

Friendly, calm authority with the front desk. Use their name, never pitch them, ask for the owner by first name.

Whatbox Digital · Appointment Setting Team

16

Gatekeepers: their job is to prevent risk, and they judge you on tone in about seven seconds. Speak like you’re supposed to be there. Friendly, first names, calm certainty, no asking permission: ‘Hey Rebecca, can you put me through to Sarah?’ Never pitch the gatekeeper; keep it above their pay grade. Never be rude. Answer and return a question: ‘It’s regarding her agency’s marketing. Is she available?’ If they’re busy: ‘When’s a better time to call back? Is there a better line to reach them directly?’ Show-ups: get a verbal commitment (‘So just to confirm, you’ll be at your desk at 4pm Tuesday, correct?’), make sure the Calendly reminders go to their cell, and do a courtesy check-in before the meeting.
